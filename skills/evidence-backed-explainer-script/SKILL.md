# Evidence-Backed Explainer Script

## Purpose

Turn a topic or claim into a short, understandable script whose important factual statements can be checked and whose visuals add evidence instead of repeating the narration.

## Workflow

1. Read the project's `BRIEF.md`, `SCRIPT.md`, and `STORYBOARD.md` instructions. Confirm the audience, target duration, format, and the question the video must answer.
2. State the main claim in one sentence. Break it into factual subclaims that can be independently checked.
3. Research from primary records first: papers, datasets, filings, technical documentation, interviews, or direct statements. Use secondary reporting to find context and counterevidence, then trace important details back to primary sources where possible.
4. Keep a source ledger while researching. For each claim, record the source URL, publisher, publication date, date of the underlying data or event, and the exact detail it supports. Mark unavailable or conflicting evidence explicitly.
5. Remove claims that lack adequate support. Separate established facts from estimates, interpretation, prediction, and uncertainty. Do not turn an estimate into a precise fact.
6. Build a scene outline around the viewer's next question. Give each scene one primary reading and identify the spoken point, the visual proof, and what the viewer should understand afterward.
7. Draft natural spoken language. Attribute contested claims, preserve units and time periods, and avoid certainty that the evidence does not support.
8. Review every factual sentence against the source ledger. Add citations or source credits in the format requested by the project brief.

## Quality check

- The central claim is clear before the evidence gets detailed.
- Every material factual claim has a traceable source and the source supports the wording used.
- Data dates, publication dates, units, denominators, and comparisons are correct.
- Visuals show a mechanism, example, comparison, or source evidence; they do not merely repeat the spoken sentence.
- The ending answers the opening question without introducing an unsupported claim.
- Any meaningful limitation or unresolved uncertainty remains visible in the script or storyboard.

Do not imitate a specific creator's phrasing, use a source's distinctive graphics, or treat citations as a substitute for accurate claims.
