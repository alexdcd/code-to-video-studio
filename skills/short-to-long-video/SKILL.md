# Short-to-Long Video

## Purpose

Develop an existing short into a longer video while preserving its useful core and adding enough context, evidence, explanation, and resolution to justify the longer format.

## Workflow

1. Read `BRIEF.md`, `SCRIPT.md`, `STORYBOARD.md`, and the source project's editable timeline. Identify the short's central promise, its strongest visual moments, and what it leaves unanswered.
2. Confirm the new audience, platform, aspect ratio, target duration, and desired level of continuity with the short. Keep the new project separate from the source project.
3. Inventory source scenes and assets. Mark each as `reuse`, `expand`, `replace`, or `new`, with a reason. Reuse editable project material where available; use a flattened render only when the brief calls for it.
4. List the questions that a longer treatment must answer. Research additional claims from reliable sources, record the relevant dates and limits, and update any time-sensitive facts.
5. Build a new outline with a clear opening, context, explanation or mechanism, evidence and examples, limitations or alternatives, and a satisfying resolution. Allocate time to each section based on the target duration instead of stretching the short's existing lines.
6. Preserve the short's thesis and useful transitions, but rewrite narration for the new structure. Every reused section should advance the longer video; remove repetition that adds no new understanding.
7. In the storyboard, distinguish reused scenes from expanded and new scenes. Specify the visual reading, narration, evidence source, and sound for each scene.
8. After the new narration is final, recheck scene timing and subtitles against its actual word timings. Review the long version on its own and confirm that it works without knowledge of the short.

## Quality check

- The long video offers context or evidence the short could not fit.
- Reused material has a clear role in the new argument.
- Added facts are verified and do not depend on outdated short-form narration.
- The pacing comes from the new story structure, not repeated summaries or padded holds.
- A viewer who has not seen the short can follow the long version.
- The ending resolves the main question and does not simply restate the opening.

Do not copy a published short's exact words or distinctive visual identity when the new project does not own or license them.
